# Perplexity research brief: Build Your Pillars for AgeTech After Dark

Paste the prompt below into Perplexity (Pro or Deep Research). Then bring the answers back; each figure gets checked at its primary source before it is used.

## Prompt

I am applying to the AgeTech Collaborative's "AgeTech After Dark" pitch competition (AARP-affiliated; prompt: "How does your tech solution help make aging easier?"). My product is a gentle, private health tracker for adults 50+ (and the people who care about them). It lets someone log as little or as much as they like (water, sleep, movement, mood), never shames a missed day, treats mental health as a pillar equal to water and sleep, has no insurer or provider tie and no ads, and is meant to let a loved one follow along only if the person chooses.

Find primary-source evidence, published 2021 to 2026, for each numbered claim. For every figure give: the exact number, population, year and country, how it was measured, the sample size, the journal or publisher, a DOI or direct URL, and a short quote. Say plainly when only a secondary source exists. Do not use blogs, press releases or vendor pages. Prefer systematic reviews, national surveys (CDC, NIH/NIA, AARP Research, Pew, Census), and peer-reviewed studies.

1. Prevalence of insomnia or poor sleep in adults 65+, ideally one pooled or national figure, and how it compares with adults under 50.
2. Prevalence of low fluid intake or dehydration in adults 65+, and a statement on why thirst sensation declines with age.
3. Prevalence of depressive symptoms and loneliness in adults 65+ in the US and Canada, and rates of untreated depression.
4. Evidence that older adults are LESS likely than younger adults to be offered or to seek mental-health support (stigma, under-diagnosis).
5. Older adults' concerns about health-data privacy and sharing with insurers, advertisers or data brokers (AARP 2020 Big Health Data Survey says 67% worry how companies use data and 58% worry about medical records being shared beyond providers; find newer 2023 to 2026 equivalents).
6. Evidence that all-or-nothing or streak-based health apps cause drop-off or distress in older users, and that flexible or partial logging improves adherence.
7. Evidence on remote family caregivers (US adult children): how many provide care at a distance, and whether sharing health data with a family member improves adherence or outcomes, with the risks of surveillance and loss of autonomy.
8. Apple Health and Google Health Connect: adoption among adults 65+ in the US, and any reported barriers.
9. Market size: number of US adults 65+ and 50+ (Census 2024 or later) and smartphone ownership among 65+ (Pew 2024 or later).
10. Any rule, guideline or standard on AI and mental-health content for older adults that a product should respect (FDA wellness guidance, FTC Health Breach Notification Rule, state laws on AI mental-health chatbots enacted 2025 to 2026).

End with a table: claim number, best figure, source, link, confidence (primary / secondary / not found).

## What I will do with the answers

Check each figure against the original, drop anything I cannot verify, and use only the strongest two or three in the application. Do not pad.
